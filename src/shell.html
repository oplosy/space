      What is real here

      Planet positions use JPL’s approximate Keplerian elements (Standish), accurate to arc-minutes between 1800 and 2050 and degrading slowly outside. The Moon follows the main terms of Meeus’s lunar theory, precise enough to reproduce eclipses to within minutes. Poles and spin come from the IAU WGCCRE rotation models. Eclipse shadows, ring shadows and atmospheric scattering are computed per pixel from the actual geometry. Sizes and distances are true to scale unless you enlarge bodies.

      Moons other than Earth’s use mean circular orbits in their planet’s equatorial plane, so their phases are approximate. Small-body belts are statistical populations with real structure: Kirkwood gaps, Hildas, Jupiter’s trojans at L4/L5, and the Kuiper belt.

      Zoom far enough out and the sky gives way to a model of the Milky Way: a barred spiral with its measured bar angle, arm pitch and the Sun 26,670 light years from the centre. Near Sagittarius A* every pixel is ray-traced through Schwarzschild spacetime, so the shadow, photon ring and lensed disc come from the physics rather than a painted texture.

      Further out, the Magellanic Clouds, Andromeda and Triangulum sit at their measured distances and orientations. Beyond the Local Group, real galaxy clusters (Virgo, Fornax, Centaurus, Norma, Perseus, Hydra, Coma, Leo, Hercules, Shapley) anchor a statistical cosmic web that runs out to the cosmic microwave background, 46.5 billion light years away and shown in false colour.

      Thirty nearby and famous stars, from Proxima Centauri to Betelgeuse and VY Canis Majoris, sit at their measured distances with their true sizes and colours. From Earth they replace their catalogue points, so the sky is unchanged until you leave. Two stellar black holes are ray-traced like Sgr A*: dormant Gaia BH1, the nearest known, and Cygnus X-1 with its accretion disc and blue supergiant companion.

      Voyager 1 and 2 fly their real routes, rebuilt from the actual flyby dates and pinned to their measured positions today; set the clock to 1977 or use the flyby buttons to follow the Grand Tour. The faint shell at about 120 AU is the heliopause they have both crossed. TRAPPIST-1, 40.7 light-years away, has its seven planets on their measured orbits; the three in green orbit in the habitable zone. Their surfaces are illustrations.
